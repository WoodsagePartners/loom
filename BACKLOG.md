## Big ideas, not yet designed

### Technique enablement assets

For any given innovation technique, the library should carry not just how the engine executes it, but how a **human** brings it into a room. Four asset types per technique:

1. **How to introduce it.** The opening move. How you explain it in ninety seconds to people who have never heard of it and are slightly suspicious. Framing that lands with engineers, with executives, with crews.
2. **How to build skill in it.** Exercises, drills, warmups. Practice on something safe before pointing it at the real problem. Progressions from first exposure to competent solo use.
3. **How to facilitate with it.** The craft. Where groups get stuck, what to do when the room goes quiet, how long to run it, when to stop, common failure modes, what a good result feels like from the front of the room.
4. **How to keep it alive between sessions.** The residency problem. Prompts, nudges, small assignments and provocations that keep a client's skill and interest piqued while the facilitator is not there. This is directly the problem the whole application exists to solve, pointed at the technique level.

This turns the library from an engine configuration into a genuine methodology repository, and it is the natural body of work for a student cohort.

### Multiplayer enabled

Multiplayer **capable** is in the current spec: author fields on every node, fiber and ledger entry, participant lists on threads, no assumption of a single writer. Multiplayer **enabled** needs a backend and a sync strategy. Deferred by decision, not by oversight.

Once enabled it unlocks the things every principle already assumes: silent grouping, async contribution across rotations, anonymous divergence so the loudest title stops bending the room.

## Wish list, decided but not built

1. **Pins drift forward.** A pin's tether stays where it was tied for history, but its head rides at the live end for relevance. A thread that is behind the curve is useless.
2. **Staleness check on untie.** Three outcomes. Still live, comes back unchanged. Already answered, closes itself and points at what answered it. Still valid but stale in wording, offers a restatement against the current working question. Translation, not thinking, and the human accepts or rejects.
3. **Scaffold advances on who, not what.** Movement is "they did it without me," so the meter should respond to who ran the pull, not that a pull happened. Now folded into the scoreboard spec.
4. **Journey loops rather than marches.** Reframing currently jumps to step eight and never returns. Real threads reframe repeatedly. Steps six through nine probably cycle.
5. **Return view computes a real diff.** Currently canned.
6. **Empty threads waste the canvas.** Addressed in the spec by sizing to content, but worth checking it actually feels right in use.
7. **Scoreboard.** Specified. Keep the handwritten line as a caption over real numbers.

## Killed, with reasons

Recorded so nobody proposes them again in six months without knowing why they died.

**Solo lead generation version ("The Bench").** Not the interest. The value is collaborative, and a single player taste was a distraction from a real engagement already on the table.

**Portfolio collision detector.** Noble has three challenges. You can see the couplings yourself over a coffee. A whole feature generalised from one nice observation, which is exactly the sin the product exists to diagnose.

**Generic question generator.** Generic questions get the answers people already had ready. Every organisation is already full of questions nobody has collected. Harvest what people are privately carrying, then forge variations from human material.
