## Analogical encoding and multi-source comparison

Context: Ron proposed running analogous thinking across three or four situations rather than the conventional pairwise comparison, harvesting both what holds across all of them and what appears in only one. Question was whether this is established practice or a new contribution.

**Verdict, in short.** The abstraction half is well supported science. The singleton-as-novelty half is the more original move, and packaging the whole thing as a named instrument with a defined protocol is a legitimate codification contribution, in the same category as what Basadur did for creative problem solving.

### What the literature establishes

- Comparing two cases produces schema abstraction that studying the same two cases separately does not. Comparison forces alignment and re-representation, yielding a representation less context specific than either case alone.
- Modeling work shows sequential analogical comparison generates progressively **more abstract** representations, which supports going past two sources.
- In design practice, multiple source domains are studied, but typically as a **pool to select from** rather than a set held side by side. Designers are offered analogies from biological, cross domain and within domain sources, and researchers measure selection frequency and resulting novelty.
- Distant analogies correlate with higher originality in resulting designs. Biological and cross domain analogies both outperform within domain ones, and middle distance analogies can be as effective as far ones.

### Where Ron's version departs

Standard analogical work treats features appearing in only one case as noise to be abstracted away, because the goal is the shared schema. Ron proposes the inverse reading: a property present in one of four is a **novelty candidate**, precisely because it survived into a set where nothing else carries it. That is a different harvest from the one the literature usually describes.
